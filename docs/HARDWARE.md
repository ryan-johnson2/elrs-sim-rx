# Hardware

The current hardware is the carrier PCB. It was proven first as a hand-built prototype on a
breadboard and then perfboard, which is still in daily use.

| | What it is | State | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carrier v0.2 | 22 x 39 mm PCB. The fab fits the power switch and LED driver; the RP2040-Zero, the receiver and the button leads are soldered by hand | 5 boards ordered 2026-11-14, not yet received | [Board](../hardware/carrier/README.md), [case](../hardware/case-carrier/README.md) |
| Prototype | RP2040-Zero module, discrete power switch and LED driver, on a breadboard and then a 4 x 6 cm perfboard | Built, in daily use | [Parts and pin plan](HBOM.md), [wiring diagram](wiring.html) |

Work in progress is on the `devel` branch: a board with the RP2040 itself fitted, so only the
receiver and button are soldered by hand, plus the prototype's step-by-step build guides and
its case.

## The circuit

- **RP2040** presents a USB HID gamepad and a serial port to the PC.
- **ExpressLRS receiver** (Happymodel EP2 on the carrier; an RP3 also works on the prototype)
  talks CRSF to the RP2040 at 420000 baud on GP0 and GP1.
- **Receiver power switch** on GP2, so the dongle can stay plugged in with the receiver off:
  a TPS22918 load switch on the carrier, an AO3401A driven by a 2N7000 on the prototype.
- **Front-panel button** on GP3: short press toggles receiver power, long press binds.
- **Button LED ring** on GP4 through a small MOSFET, mirroring the receiver's status LED.
- **Status LED**: the RP2040-Zero's RGB LED on GP16 shows the same pattern.

Without the button, the receiver simply powers up with the dongle. A module wired straight to
a receiver with four wires (5V, GND, GP0, GP1) runs the lite firmware build and needs none of
the above.

Pin assignments are in [HBOM.md](HBOM.md) and `firmware/src/board.h`. What the LED patterns
mean, and their limits, is in [LED-BEHAVIOUR.md](LED-BEHAVIOUR.md).

## Bringing up a new board

[BRINGUP.md](BRINGUP.md) is the checklist: module alone, then button and ring, then receiver,
then the power switch, then bind, passthrough and a soak.

## Tools

Everything runs in containers; nothing but Docker is needed on the host.

| Task | Command | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Regenerate the PCB, run ERC and DRC, render it | `tools/kicad.sh hardware/carrier/gen/build.sh` | `hardware/carrier/out/` |
| JLCPCB order files | `tools/kicad.sh hardware/carrier/gen/fab.sh` | `hardware/carrier/out/fab/` |
| Export the case and check it for overlaps | `hardware/case-carrier/build.sh` | `hardware/case-carrier/out/` |
| Wiring diagram PDF | see the comment at the top of `docs/wiring.html` | `docs/WIRING.pdf` |

The schematic and board are generated by the Python scripts in `hardware/carrier/gen/`, with
placement and routing written out as coordinates. The `.kicad_sch` and `.kicad_pcb` files are
committed so the design opens directly in KiCad 9; the `out/` folders are not.

## What it costs

Actual figures for the first carrier order are in the change log at the end of
[HBOM.md](HBOM.md): $120 landed for five assembled boards, most of it fixed assembly, shipping
and duty charges. A finished unit came to roughly $47 with the receiver, module, button and
case, against an estimated $22 to $27 for the perfboard build.
