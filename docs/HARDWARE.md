their source; open the files in a browser, or use the copies the hosted config page serves
under `guides/`.

## The circuit

All three are the same electrically.

- **RP2040** presents a USB HID gamepad and a serial port to the PC.
- **ExpressLRS receiver** (Happymodel EP2 on the PCBs; an RP3 also works on the prototype)
  talks CRSF to the RP2040 at 420000 baud on GP0 and GP1.
- **Receiver power switch** on GP2, so the dongle can stay plugged in with the receiver off:
  an AO3401A driven by a 2N7000 on the prototype, a TPS22918 load switch on the PCBs.
- **Front-panel button** on GP3: short press toggles receiver power, long press binds.
- **Button LED ring** on GP4 through a small MOSFET, mirroring the receiver's status LED.
- **Status LED**: the RP2040-Zero's RGB LED on GP16, or a plain LED on GP25 on the integrated
  board. The firmware drives both.

Without the button, the receiver simply powers up with the dongle. A module wired straight to
a receiver with four wires (5V, GND, GP0, GP1) runs the lite firmware build and needs none of
the above.

Pin assignments are in [HBOM.md](HBOM.md) and `firmware/src/board.h`. What the LED patterns
mean, and their limits, is in [LED-BEHAVIOUR.md](LED-BEHAVIOUR.md).

## Bringing up a new board

[BRINGUP.md](BRINGUP.md) is the checklist: module alone, then button and ring, then receiver,
then the power switch, then bind, passthrough and a soak. The breadboard guide walks through
the same stages with the wiring for each.

## Tools

Everything runs in containers; nothing but Docker is needed on the host.

| Task | Command | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Regenerate a PCB, run ERC and DRC, render it | `tools/kicad.sh hardware/carrier/gen/build.sh` (or `hardware/board/gen/build.sh`) | `hardware/<board>/out/` |
| JLCPCB order files for the carrier | `tools/kicad.sh hardware/carrier/gen/fab.sh` | `hardware/carrier/out/fab/` |
| Export a case and check it for overlaps | `hardware/case-carrier/build.sh` (or `hardware/case-proto/build.sh`) | `hardware/<case>/out/` |
| Wiring diagram PDF | see the comment at the top of `docs/wiring.html` | `docs/WIRING.pdf` |

The schematics and boards are generated by the Python scripts in each board's `gen/` folder,
with placement written out as coordinates. The `.kicad_sch` and `.kicad_pcb` files are
committed so the designs open directly in KiCad 9; the `out/` folders are not.

## What it costs

Actual figures for the first carrier order are in the change log at the end of
[HBOM.md](HBOM.md): $120 landed for five assembled boards, most of it fixed assembly, shipping
and duty charges. A finished unit came to roughly $47 with the receiver, module, button and
case, against an estimated $22 to $27 for the perfboard build.
